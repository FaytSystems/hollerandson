Tattoo appointment guide

The right tattoo starts with the right conversation.

Bring an idea worth discussing and give the artist the details they need to respond. Here is a clear path from browsing portfolios to a confirmed studio appointment.

By Holler & Son · Published October 28, 2026

Choose an artist by their work

Start with portfolios rather than a booking slot. Look for examples that resemble the style, scale, and level of detail you want. A studio may have several artists with different specialties, so identify the person whose work is closest to your idea.

Holler & Son studio profiles bring together artwork, artists, specialties, location, and contact information. Use those details to create a short list of studios you would actually visit. Location matters when a project could involve more than one conversation or session.

Send a brief the studio can act on

A useful first message explains the idea without asking the artist to guess the basics. Include the subject, preferred style, approximate size, placement, and the time frame you have in mind. If your dates are flexible, say so. Tell the studio whether you prefer an email or phone reply.

References help communicate a direction. Explain what you like about them: the line work, composition, shading, color, or overall mood. That gives the artist something to discuss beyond an image alone.

A simple request outline

“I am interested in [subject] in a [style] direction, approximately [size], for [placement]. I like [specific elements] in your portfolio. My preferred dates are [dates], and I can also consider [alternatives]. Please contact me by [email or phone] about the next step.”

Replace each bracket with your own details. The studio can then explain whether the project fits the artist's work and which information is needed next.

A request starts the booking process

Sending an appointment request is different from receiving a confirmed booking. Wait for the studio's response and check the agreed artist, date, location, and next steps. For a larger or more detailed design, the studio may want a consultation first.

Discuss the quote, what it includes, and any deposit or cancellation terms directly with the studio. Keep the written confirmation so you can refer back to the arrangement. Holler & Son's request flow connects you with the studio; the studio confirms the appointment details and payment requirements.

For studio owners: make the first reply easier

A clear portfolio and complete profile help clients send better inquiries. Keep your artists, specialties, contact details, and studio information current. When a request arrives, respond with a concrete next step: a question about the brief, consultation instructions, or a proposed appointment.

Common booking questions

Do I need a finished design to contact a studio?

A clear description and relevant references can start the conversation. Ask the studio how its artist prefers to develop the design.

Can I request a particular artist?

Name the artist in your message and explain which portfolio pieces match the direction you want.

How do I know my appointment is confirmed?

Look for an explicit confirmation from the studio, including the date and any steps you need to complete.
